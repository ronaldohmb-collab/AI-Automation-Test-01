# Experimental Conditions

## Experiment

**AI Automation Test #1 — Brand Awareness & Market Entry**

## Purpose

The experimental conditions were established to ensure that the three process configurations could be compared against a consistent reference.

The intent was to isolate the effect of changing the **operating process** rather than changing the underlying campaign objective or requirements.

---

## Controlled Reference

The experiment used a defined **Brand Awareness & Market Entry Campaign** based on the process:

> **Building brand awareness through digital integration**

The campaign requirements and reference dataset were treated as the common basis for the three comparative runs.

---

## Constant Conditions

The following elements were held consistent across the experiment where applicable:

* Core campaign objective
* Reference campaign requirements
* Reference dataset
* Intended marketing outcome
* Defined process scope
* Required strategic considerations
* Evaluation criteria
* Human accountability requirements
* Evidence boundaries

The purpose of maintaining these conditions was to ensure that differences between the runs primarily reflected differences in the **operating approach**.

---

## Variable Condition

The principal variable was the way in which the process was organised and executed.

### Run A

Human-led manual operating process.

### Run B

AI-engineered operating process with deliberate allocation of selected activities to AI.

### Run C

Optimised AI-enabled operating process incorporating additional safeguards, clearer human-AI boundaries, and learning mechanisms.

---

## Human Authority

Human authority remained a controlled component of the experiment.

AI integration was not treated as permission to remove human responsibility from strategic or consequential decisions.

Human involvement remained relevant where decisions required:

* strategic judgement;
* accountability;
* interpretation of ambiguous information;
* risk assessment;
* brand judgement;
* approval;
* or intervention when evidence or confidence was insufficient.

---

## AI Role

AI was evaluated as an operating capability rather than as an autonomous replacement for the marketing function.

The experiment considered three broad forms of AI involvement:

### Automation

AI performing defined activities under predetermined conditions and controls.

### Augmentation

AI increasing human capacity while a human remains responsible for judgement or approval.

### Human-led

Activities where human authority, judgement, or accountability remains primary.

---

## Safeguard Conditions

The optimised process incorporated safeguards intended to prevent operational efficiency from overriding quality, governance, or strategic control.

These included consideration of:

* audience relevance;
* evidence quality;
* confidence;
* brand protection;
* causality;
* missing data;
* publication controls;
* budget controls;
* human approval;
* and learning feedback.

---

## Time Conditions

The manual control process was measured at approximately **464 minutes** across the defined stages.

The AI-engineered process was modelled at approximately **107 minutes**.

The optimised process was modelled at approximately **108 minutes**.

The time comparison was used as an indicator of potential capacity recovery.

It was not treated as proof of equivalent real-world productivity because the AI-enabled configurations were not validated through live production execution.

---

## Quality Conditions

Efficiency was not considered sufficient evidence of a successful process.

The AI-enabled configurations were expected to maintain acceptable standards of:

* strategic quality;
* decision quality;
* evidence quality;
* human oversight;
* brand integrity;
* and risk management.

Where an efficiency gain created an unacceptable reduction in these areas, the gain would not automatically be considered beneficial.

---

## Learning Condition

The experiment included **Stage 14 — Updated Learning Informing Future Decision-Making**.

The learning stage was designed to test whether observations from the process could be incorporated into future decision-making and process improvements.

This established learning as an explicit operating condition rather than an informal post-campaign activity.

---

## Evidence Boundary

The experiment was conducted as a controlled/modelled test.

The following were therefore outside the validated scope of this experiment:

* live production execution;
* live API reliability;
* software/runtime failure behaviour;
* real-world system latency;
* production-scale error rates;
* actual commercial performance;
* actual revenue impact;
* and long-term operational sustainability.

These areas remain candidates for future validation.

---

## Replication Principle

Future experiments should preserve the distinction between:

**Controlled variables**

The conditions that should remain consistent to enable comparison.

**Process variables**

The elements deliberately changed to test a different operating configuration.

**Outcome measures**

The dimensions used to determine whether the process changed meaningfully.

This distinction is intended to reduce uncontrolled changes between future experiments and improve comparability across the wider testing programme.
