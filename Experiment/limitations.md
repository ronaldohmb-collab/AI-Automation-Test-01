# Limitations

## Experiment

**AI Automation Test #1 — Brand Awareness & Market Entry**

## Purpose of This Document

This document defines the boundaries of what can and cannot be concluded from the experiment.

The experiment produced useful evidence regarding process design and potential capacity effects. However, several limitations prevent the results from being interpreted as proof of production performance or commercial impact.

---

## 1. AI-Enabled Runs Were Modelled

The most significant limitation is that **Run B and Run C were controlled/modelled configurations rather than live production executions**.

Their estimated process times therefore represent the expected operating requirements of the redesigned processes under the defined experimental conditions.

They do not establish that the same time savings would occur consistently in a live operating environment.

---

## 2. The 77% Reduction Is Not a Validated Productivity Claim

The comparison between the manual baseline and the AI-enabled configurations indicated an estimated reduction of approximately **77% in process time**.

However, this should not be presented as:

> "AI increased productivity by 77%."

The result is a controlled experimental finding based on the defined process and modelled AI-enabled configurations.

Real-world productivity may differ because of factors such as:

* system latency;
* API failures;
* integration complexity;
* human review time;
* unexpected exceptions;
* rework;
* data quality;
* tool limitations;
* and operational interruptions.

---

## 3. No Live API or Runtime Validation

The experiment did not establish how the proposed AI-enabled process would behave when implemented through live software integrations or APIs.

Consequently, it does not validate:

* API reliability;
* authentication failures;
* rate limits;
* service outages;
* integration failures;
* unexpected outputs;
* processing delays;
* or runtime recovery mechanisms.

These require a subsequent implementation and validation stage.

---

## 4. No Production-Scale Testing

The experiment was conducted under controlled conditions rather than at production scale.

It therefore does not establish how the operating model would perform with:

* larger datasets;
* multiple campaigns;
* multiple stakeholders;
* competing priorities;
* larger volumes of content;
* larger audiences;
* or simultaneous workflows.

---

## 5. No Commercial Outcome Validation

The experiment measured process and operating characteristics.

It did not establish actual changes in:

* revenue;
* leads;
* conversion;
* customer acquisition cost;
* brand awareness;
* market share;
* or other commercial outcomes.

The experiment therefore cannot be used to claim that AI automation directly produces improved business performance.

---

## 6. Quality Was Evaluated Within the Experimental Framework

The experiment included quality, control, risk, and governance considerations.

However, quality assessment within a controlled experiment is not equivalent to long-term validation in a live commercial environment.

Future testing should examine whether quality remains stable when the process is exposed to:

* real audiences;
* real campaign feedback;
* unexpected inputs;
* operational pressure;
* and repeated execution.

---

## 7. Human Review Requirements May Change in Production

The experimental process identified areas where human judgement and authority should remain.

However, the actual amount of human review required in production may be higher or lower than the modelled process suggests.

This could affect the practical capacity benefit.

Future validation should therefore measure **actual human intervention time**, including:

* review;
* correction;
* approval;
* exception handling;
* and rework.

---

## 8. Learning Has Not Yet Been Validated Longitudinally

Stage 14 introduced a structured mechanism for incorporating learning into future decisions.

The experiment demonstrated the design of this feedback mechanism, but it did not establish its effectiveness over multiple real-world cycles.

Longitudinal testing is required to determine whether:

* learning is captured consistently;
* learning improves subsequent decisions;
* incorrect learning can be detected;
* outdated learning is removed;
* and human governance remains effective over time.

---

## 9. Generalisability Is Not Yet Established

The experiment tested one defined marketing process.

It cannot yet establish that the same results would occur across:

* other marketing disciplines;
* different organisations;
* different campaign types;
* different datasets;
* different AI systems;
* or different levels of process complexity.

This is one reason subsequent experiments are required.

---

## 10. The Experiment Does Not Demonstrate AI Replacement of Marketing Professionals

The experiment was explicitly designed around **human-AI collaboration**, not replacement.

The findings support investigation into how AI can potentially reduce operational workload and increase human capacity.

They do not demonstrate that human marketing expertise, strategic judgement, accountability, or governance can be removed from the process.

---

## Overall Evidence Boundary

The experiment provides evidence of:

* controlled process comparison;
* potential capacity recovery;
* possible AI-enabled task redistribution;
* the importance of human-AI responsibility boundaries;
* the value of explicit safeguards;
* and the feasibility of incorporating learning into an AI-enabled process design.

It does **not** provide sufficient evidence to claim:

* validated production productivity gains;
* reliable live automation;
* commercial performance improvement;
* long-term sustainability;
* or generalised AI effectiveness across marketing.

---

## Implications for Future Testing

These limitations directly inform the next stages of the testing programme.

Future experiments should progressively move from:

> **Controlled modelling**

toward:

> **Live execution → measurement → validation → repeated execution → longitudinal learning**

The purpose of subsequent testing is therefore not simply to reproduce the 77% result, but to determine **which elements of the redesigned operating model remain effective when exposed to real-world conditions**.
