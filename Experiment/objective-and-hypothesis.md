# Objectives and Hypothesis

## Experiment

**AI Automation Test #1 — Brand Awareness & Market Entry**

## Primary Objective

To determine whether an AI-engineered marketing operating process can significantly reduce low-value operational work and recover human capacity while maintaining strategic quality, appropriate human control, and defined safeguards.

The experiment was designed to evaluate AI at the **process level**, rather than simply testing whether AI could perform individual marketing tasks.

## Secondary Objectives

The experiment also sought to:

1. Identify which activities within the marketing process could be automated responsibly.
2. Identify which activities were better suited to AI-assisted human execution.
3. Identify decisions that should remain under human authority.
4. Determine whether AI integration could reduce process time without making efficiency the sole optimisation objective.
5. Establish safeguards for areas where errors, weak evidence, inappropriate automation, or loss of human judgement could create risk.
6. Examine whether learning from the process could be incorporated into future decision-making.
7. Establish a repeatable framework that could be applied to subsequent AI automation tests.

## Research Question

> Can an AI-engineered marketing operating process significantly reduce operational workload while maintaining the quality, control, and strategic integrity of the marketing process?

## Hypothesis

> An AI-engineered marketing operating process will materially reduce the amount of operational work and human time required to complete the defined marketing process, while maintaining acceptable strategic and quality controls when appropriate human oversight and safeguards are retained.

## Null Hypothesis

> Integrating AI into the defined marketing operating process will not produce a meaningful reduction in operational workload and/or will result in unacceptable deterioration of quality, strategic control, or governance.

## What the Experiment Was Intended to Establish

The experiment was not designed to establish that AI can replace marketing professionals.

It was designed to investigate whether marketing work could be **restructured around human-AI collaboration** so that:

* repetitive and lower-value operational work could potentially be automated;
* AI could augment human analysis and execution;
* humans could retain authority over strategic, consequential, or risk-sensitive decisions; and
* learning from outcomes could be incorporated into future process decisions.

## Success Criteria

The experiment would be considered informative if it could:

1. Establish a comparable manual baseline.
2. Demonstrate a measurable difference in process workload between the manual and AI-enabled configurations.
3. Identify where AI could perform work without requiring equivalent human effort.
4. Identify where human intervention remained necessary.
5. Define meaningful safeguards around AI-enabled work.
6. Produce sufficient evidence to determine what should be tested or validated in subsequent experiments.

A reduction in process time alone would **not** be considered sufficient evidence of success.

Quality, control, risk, and evidence boundaries were considered alongside efficiency.
