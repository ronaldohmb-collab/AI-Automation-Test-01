# Methodology

## Experiment

**AI Automation Test #1 — Brand Awareness & Market Entry**

## Experimental Approach

The experiment used a controlled comparative approach to examine how the same defined marketing process changed under three operating configurations:

1. **Run A — Manual Control Process**
2. **Run B — AI-Engineered Process**
3. **Run C — Optimised Process**

The purpose of using multiple runs was to establish a baseline, introduce deliberate AI integration, and then refine the resulting process based on identified weaknesses, safeguards, and learning.

The experiment was therefore designed as a **process comparison**, rather than a comparison of individual AI tools.

---

## Reference Process

The process tested was:

> **Building brand awareness through digital integration**

The process was structured as a controlled **Brand Awareness & Market Entry Campaign**.

The same underlying campaign requirements and reference dataset were maintained across the comparative runs to provide a consistent basis for evaluation.

---

## Run A — Manual Control

Run A established the baseline process.

The process was performed using a conventional human-led operating approach, with the defined marketing activities completed manually.

The purpose of Run A was to establish:

* the sequence of activities;
* the operational workload;
* the time required across the defined stages;
* the decisions requiring human involvement; and
* the baseline against which AI-enabled configurations could be evaluated.

Run A required approximately **464 minutes** across the measured stages.

---

## Run B — AI-Engineered Process

Run B introduced deliberate AI integration into the same underlying process.

The objective was not simply to ask AI to perform isolated tasks. Instead, the process itself was redesigned to determine:

* which activities could be delegated to AI;
* which activities could be accelerated through AI assistance;
* where humans still needed to review or approve outputs;
* where safeguards were required; and
* where human strategic authority needed to remain intact.

Run B was modelled at approximately **107 minutes**.

This represented a substantial theoretical reduction in process workload compared with the manual control.

---

## Run C — Optimised Process

Run C refined the AI-engineered process after examining the weaknesses and control requirements identified during the earlier configuration.

The optimisation process incorporated:

* additional safeguards;
* clearer human-AI responsibility boundaries;
* evidence and confidence considerations;
* brand protection controls;
* publication and budget controls;
* consideration of causality and missing data;
* and a structured learning mechanism.

Run C was modelled at approximately **108 minutes**.

The slight increase relative to Run B was treated as acceptable where additional controls improved governance or reduced risk.

The objective of Run C was therefore **not to achieve the lowest possible process time**, but to establish a more balanced operating model.

---

## Stage 14 — Updated Learning Informing Future Decision-Making

A dedicated learning stage was introduced into the optimised process.

**Stage 14 — Updated Learning Informing Future Decision-Making** was designed to ensure that observations, errors, outcomes, and other relevant learning could be incorporated into future decisions and process improvements.

This was intended to create a feedback loop rather than treating each campaign as an isolated execution.

The inclusion of this stage reflects the broader principle that an AI-enabled operating system should be capable of **learning from controlled outcomes while remaining subject to human governance**.

---

## Evaluation Framework

The runs were evaluated across multiple dimensions rather than time alone.

These included:

### 1. Capacity

How much human time and operational effort was required to complete the process?

### 2. Quality

Did the process maintain an acceptable standard of strategic, analytical, creative, and executional quality?

### 3. Control

Did humans retain appropriate authority over strategic and consequential decisions?

### 4. Risk

Were appropriate safeguards present to manage errors, weak evidence, inappropriate outputs, or other potential failure modes?

### 5. Learning

Could observations from the process be captured and incorporated into future decision-making?

### 6. Reusability

Could the resulting operating approach be applied as a repeatable process rather than functioning only as a one-off experiment?

---

## Time Measurement

Process time was recorded or modelled across the defined stages of each run.

The resulting comparison was:

| Run   | Configuration  | Approximate Process Time |
| ----- | -------------- | -----------------------: |
| Run A | Manual Control |              464 minutes |
| Run B | AI-Engineered  |              107 minutes |
| Run C | Optimised      |              108 minutes |

Compared with the manual baseline, the AI-engineered configuration represented an estimated **77% reduction in process time**.

The optimised configuration represented an estimated reduction of approximately **77%** as well.

These figures represent the controlled/modelled experiment and should not be interpreted as validated production productivity gains.

---

## Quality and Control Assessment

The experiment deliberately avoided treating efficiency as the sole measure of success.

The AI-enabled configurations were assessed alongside requirements for:

* human judgement;
* strategic accountability;
* evidence quality;
* brand safety;
* appropriate intervention;
* governance;
* and learning.

This was intended to prevent the optimisation process from simply selecting the fastest configuration regardless of quality or risk.

---

## Evidence Classification

The experiment consists of:

* a controlled manual baseline;
* AI-engineered process modelling;
* an optimised process model;
* structured comparison;
* documented safeguards;
* and defined evidence boundaries.

Runs B and C were **controlled/modelled configurations rather than live production executions**.

Consequently, the experiment provides evidence of **process-design potential and controlled capacity effects**, but does not establish production reliability or commercial outcomes.

---

## Reproducibility

The experiment was designed so that the methodology could be reused for subsequent tests.

Future experiments can apply the same general structure:

> **Baseline → AI Integration → Optimisation → Safeguards → Evaluation → Learning → Validation**

This creates the foundation for a repeatable AI automation testing methodology rather than treating the experiment as an isolated demonstration.
