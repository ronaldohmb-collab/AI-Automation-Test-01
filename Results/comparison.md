# Run Comparison

## Experiment

**AI Automation Test #1 — Brand Awareness & Market Entry**

## Purpose

This document compares the three experimental configurations used in AI Automation Test #1.

The purpose is to determine how the operating process changed when moving from:

> **Manual → AI-Engineered → Optimised AI-Enabled**

The comparison considers process time alongside quality, control, risk, and learning.

---

## Overall Comparison

| Run       | Configuration        | Approx. Process Time | Evidence Status       |
| --------- | -------------------- | -------------------: | --------------------- |
| **Run A** | Manual Control       |          464 minutes | Controlled baseline   |
| **Run B** | AI-Engineered        |          107 minutes | Controlled / modelled |
| **Run C** | Optimised AI-Enabled |          108 minutes | Controlled / modelled |

---

## Process-Time Comparison

Run A established the manual baseline at approximately:

> **464 minutes**

Run B reduced the modelled process requirement to approximately:

> **107 minutes**

Run C was modelled at approximately:

> **108 minutes**

Relative to Run A:

* Run B represented an estimated **77.0% reduction** in process time.
* Run C represented an estimated **76.7% reduction**, rounded to approximately **77%**.

The results therefore indicate that the majority of the modelled capacity benefit introduced through AI engineering was retained after optimisation.

---

## Why Run C Was Slightly Slower

At first glance, Run B appears marginally more efficient because it required approximately one minute less than Run C.

However, the purpose of Run C was not to minimise process time.

Run C introduced additional controls relating to:

* evidence;
* confidence;
* audience relevance;
* brand protection;
* causality;
* missing data;
* publication;
* budget;
* human approval;
* and learning.

The additional process time was therefore treated as a potential **governance cost**, rather than automatically as a failure of optimisation.

This distinction is important because removing all human checks could potentially produce a faster process while simultaneously increasing risk.

---

## Capacity Effect

The comparison indicates a substantial potential reduction in the amount of process time required under the AI-enabled configurations.

The difference between the manual baseline and the optimised process was approximately:

> **356 minutes**

This is equivalent to approximately:

> **5 hours and 56 minutes**

of modelled process capacity recovered per experimental execution.

However, this should be understood as **modelled process capacity**, not validated production productivity.

---

## Human Capacity Reallocation

The experiment suggests that the potential value of the time reduction is not simply that work finishes sooner.

Recovered capacity could potentially be redirected toward activities where human judgement creates greater value, including:

* strategic interpretation;
* creative direction;
* stakeholder engagement;
* risk assessment;
* decision-making;
* and higher-value problem solving.

The experiment therefore evaluates capacity recovery as a potential strategic benefit rather than treating time reduction as the final outcome.

## Strategic Interpretation

The three runs demonstrate an evolution in the operating model.

### Run A — Manual Control

The process relied heavily on human capacity.

This provided direct judgement and control but required substantial operational effort.

### Run B — AI-Engineered

AI integration substantially reduced the modelled process workload.

However, introducing AI also exposed additional risks and highlighted the need for clearer responsibility boundaries.

### Run C — Optimised

The process retained almost all of the modelled capacity benefit while adding explicit safeguards and a structured learning mechanism.

The result was not the fastest possible configuration.

It was the configuration judged to provide a better balance between:

> **Capacity + Quality + Control + Risk + Learning**

---

## The 77% Finding

The headline finding from the experiment is:

> **AI-enabled process redesign reduced the modelled process time by approximately 77% compared with the manual control.**

This finding should always be presented with its evidence boundary.

It means:

> Under the defined experimental conditions, the redesigned process required approximately 77% less modelled process time.

It does **not** mean:

> A business will automatically become 77% more productive by implementing AI.

The distinction is critical.

---

## What Changed Across the Runs

The experiment demonstrated three different approaches to marketing operations:

**Run A**

Human effort was the primary operating mechanism.

**Run B**

AI was introduced deliberately to reduce operational workload.

**Run C**

AI integration was refined through explicit safeguards, human authority, and learning.

The progression can therefore be summarised as:

> **Manual execution → AI-enabled efficiency → Governed AI-enabled optimisation**

---

## Evidence Boundary

The comparison provides controlled evidence of differences between the three process configurations.

It does not establish:

* production productivity;
* live system reliability;
* commercial performance;
* revenue impact;
* or long-term sustainability.

Runs B and C remain controlled/modelled configurations until validated through live execution.

---

## Key Result

The experiment's most important result was not simply the approximately 77% reduction in modelled process time.

The more significant result was that **the capacity benefit remained substantial after governance and human-control mechanisms were introduced**.

This supports further testing of AI-enabled operating processes under increasingly realistic conditions.
