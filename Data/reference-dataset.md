# Reference Dataset V1.0

## Experiment

**AI Automation Test #1 — Brand Awareness & Market Entry**

## Dataset Purpose

Dataset V1.0 provided the common reference basis for the three experimental configurations:

* **Run A — Manual Control**
* **Run B — AI-Engineered**
* **Run C — Optimised**

The dataset was designed to keep the underlying campaign conditions consistent while allowing the **operating process** to change.

The dataset was therefore treated as a controlled experimental input rather than as a source of live commercial performance data.

---

## Business Context

The organisation represented in the test was **Brand Intelligence Agency (BIA)**, a South African strategic marketing consultancy.

BIA's defined service areas were:

* Brand & Positioning
* Customer Acquisition
* Marketing Operations

The operating philosophy was:

> **Diagnose before activity.**

The BIA positioning used within the dataset included:

> **Technology expands capability. Intelligence determines what we do with that capability.**

---

## Target Market

The defined market was:

> **South African SMEs and mid-market businesses**

The campaign was designed around reaching relevant business decision-makers rather than maximising undifferentiated awareness.

---

## Campaign Objective

The primary campaign objective was to build **relevant brand awareness for BIA** among South African SME and mid-market decision-makers.

The experiment explicitly treated raw awareness or reach as insufficient on its own.

The quality and relevance of the audience were therefore part of the evaluation.

---

## Primary Audience

The primary decision-maker audience was:

> **Founder / Managing Director**

A secondary commercial audience included:

> **Commercial / Growth decision-makers**

A further secondary audience included:

> **Marketing Managers**

The audience structure was important because the experiment included scenarios where high attention or reach could nevertheless represent poor commercial relevance.

---

## Campaign Duration

The controlled campaign was structured around a:

> **Four-week campaign period**

This provided a defined time horizon for planning, execution, monitoring, reporting, and learning.

---

## Paid Media Constraint

The campaign included a maximum paid-media budget of:

> **R5,000**

The budget constraint was treated as a fixed experimental condition.

Budget decisions remained subject to appropriate human control.

---

## Resource Constraint

The operating environment assumed:

> **One primary marketer**

This constraint was important because the experiment was specifically concerned with the allocation of limited human capacity.

The test therefore examined how AI could potentially change the amount and type of work requiring human involvement.

---

## Channel Environment

The primary channel was:

> **LinkedIn**

The supporting channels were:

* Website
* Email
* Referrals

Facebook and Instagram were treated as minimal-support channels rather than primary campaign channels.

---

## Core Campaign Message

The reference campaign used the central message:

> **You don't need more marketing. You need to know what's actually holding it back.**

This message aligned with BIA's diagnostic positioning and the broader principle of identifying the underlying problem before increasing marketing activity.

---

## Reference Process

The process tested was:

> **Building brand awareness through digital integration**

The campaign was structured across 13 operational stages:

1. Objective and baseline
2. Audience
3. Market research
4. Competitor research
5. Messaging and strategic direction
6. Channel strategy
7. Content planning
8. Content creation
9. Review and approval
10. Scheduling and publishing
11. Monitoring and data collection
12. Reporting, analysis and recommendations
13. Present, learn and iterate

A separate **Stage 14 — Updated Learning Informing Future Decision-Making** was subsequently introduced into the optimised operating process.

---

## Synthetic Campaign Data

The experiment used a controlled synthetic four-week campaign dataset.

The purpose of the synthetic data was to provide comparable inputs and scenarios for the three runs without representing live BIA commercial performance.

The dataset included:

* campaign performance signals;
* audience information;
* market signals;
* competitor inputs;
* and deliberately constructed scenarios requiring interpretation.

---

## Decision Traps and Evaluation Signals

The dataset included scenarios designed to test whether the process could distinguish between useful information and misleading or incomplete signals.

Examples included:

### P05 — Vanity Metric

A performance signal that could appear positive when viewed through a surface-level metric but did not necessarily demonstrate meaningful commercial or strategic value.

### P10 — Audience Mismatch

A high-attention or high-reach result involving the wrong audience.

One example involved approximately **11,200 reach but only 2 meaningful responses**.

The purpose was to test whether the process would incorrectly treat reach as success without considering audience relevance.

### P17 — Commercial Relevance

A scenario involving approximately:

> **4,500 reach and 28 meaningful responses**

The signal was intended to test whether the process could recognise meaningful response quality rather than simply prioritising the largest reach figure.

### P15 — Competitor Spike

A competitor performance spike requiring interpretation rather than automatic imitation or reaction.

The purpose was to test whether the process could distinguish between an observed competitor change and a justified strategic response.

### P18 — Incomplete Audience Data

A scenario involving incomplete audience information.

The purpose was to test whether the process would recognise missing information rather than silently filling gaps with assumptions.

### P20 — Off-Brand AI Content

A polished AI-generated output that was nevertheless inconsistent with the intended brand.

The purpose was to test whether the process would recognise that technical or aesthetic quality does not automatically equal brand quality.

---

## Evidence and Source Conditions

The experiment included explicit evidence considerations.

The process was expected to distinguish between:

* available evidence;
* inferred information;
* uncertain information;
* missing information;
* and unsupported conclusions.

A source hierarchy and evidence labels were included as part of the dataset design.

This was intended to reduce the risk of AI-generated or human-generated assumptions being treated as established facts.

---

## Controlled Conditions

The following were treated as fixed reference conditions across the comparative runs where applicable:

* BIA business context;
* service offering;
* positioning;
* target market;
* campaign objective;
* audience structure;
* campaign duration;
* paid-media constraint;
* resource constraint;
* channel environment;
* core campaign message;
* reference process;
* dataset inputs;
* evaluation criteria;
* and evidence boundaries.

The primary experimental variable was the **operating configuration used to process the same underlying campaign requirements**.

---

## Dataset Freeze

Dataset V1.0 was treated as a frozen reference dataset for Test #1.

The purpose of freezing the dataset was to prevent the inputs from changing between runs in a way that could confound the comparison.

Changes to the underlying campaign inputs would therefore represent a change in experimental conditions rather than a continuation of the same controlled comparison.

---

## Evidence Classification

Dataset V1.0 should be understood as a **controlled experimental dataset**.

It is not a record of live BIA campaign performance and should not be interpreted as evidence of actual market, customer, competitor, or revenue outcomes.

Its purpose was to provide a consistent reference environment in which the three operating configurations could be compared.

---

## Role in the Experiment

Dataset V1.0 provided the common foundation for evaluating whether changing the **way marketing work was organised and executed** could affect:

* process workload;
* human capacity;
* quality;
* decision-making;
* control;
* safeguards;
* and learning.

This allowed the experiment to focus on the operating process rather than changing the underlying campaign problem between runs.
