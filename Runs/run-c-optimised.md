# Run C — Optimised Process

## Experiment

**AI Automation Test #1 — Brand Awareness & Market Entry**

## Run Configuration

**Run:** C
**Configuration:** Optimised AI-Enabled Process
**Purpose:** Refine the AI-engineered process by balancing capacity recovery with quality, governance, human authority, safeguards, and learning.

---

## Process Objective

Run C retained the same underlying objective as Runs A and B:

> **Building brand awareness through digital integration**

The purpose of Run C was not to achieve the lowest possible process time.

Instead, it was to determine whether the AI-engineered process could be improved by introducing clearer controls and safeguards while maintaining a substantial reduction in operational workload.

This established a more balanced optimisation objective:

> **Maximise useful human capacity without compromising strategic judgement, quality, accountability, or governance.**

---

## Starting Point

Run B demonstrated that deliberate AI integration could substantially reduce the modelled process workload.

| Run   | Configuration  | Approximate Time |
| ----- | -------------- | ---------------: |
| Run A | Manual Control |      464 minutes |
| Run B | AI-Engineered  |      107 minutes |
| Run C | Optimised      |      108 minutes |

Run C therefore introduced additional controls while accepting a small increase in modelled process time.

---

## Why Optimisation Was Required

Run B demonstrated that significant capacity could potentially be recovered through AI integration.

However, optimisation based solely on time reduction would create a risk of treating efficiency as the primary objective.

The experiment therefore examined where additional controls were justified even when they introduced some additional process effort.

This resulted in the principle:

> **A slightly slower process can be better if it materially improves control, quality, or risk management.**

---

## Optimisation Changes

The AI-engineered process was refined through clearer separation of responsibilities between AI and humans.

The optimisation incorporated:

* stronger human-AI responsibility boundaries;
* explicit human approval points;
* evidence and confidence considerations;
* audience relevance checks;
* brand protection;
* causality considerations;
* missing-data considerations;
* publication controls;
* budget controls;
* and structured learning.

These controls were designed to ensure that process efficiency did not override strategic or governance requirements.

---

## Human-AI Responsibility

The optimised process established a clearer distinction between activities that could be automated, activities that could be augmented, and activities requiring human authority.

### AI Automation

Suitable activities could be performed by AI when predefined conditions and controls were satisfied.

### AI Augmentation

AI could support analysis, preparation, interpretation, and other activities where human judgement remained responsible for the final decision.

### Human Authority

Humans retained authority over:

* strategic decisions;
* ambiguous situations;
* consequential decisions;
* risk-sensitive judgements;
* brand decisions;
* final approval;
* and situations where evidence or confidence was insufficient.

This boundary was treated as part of the operating architecture rather than as an informal instruction.

---

## Safeguards

The optimised process incorporated explicit safeguards around areas where AI-enabled efficiency could create unacceptable risk.

### Audience Relevance

AI-generated recommendations should remain relevant to the intended audience and campaign objective.

### Evidence and Confidence

Outputs should not automatically be treated as reliable simply because they are produced by an AI system.

Where evidence was insufficient or confidence was low, human review or additional investigation was required.

### Brand Protection

AI-generated outputs remained subject to human review where brand positioning, tone, reputation, or public-facing communication could be affected.

### Causality

The process recognised that correlation or observed changes should not automatically be interpreted as proof that a particular marketing activity caused the outcome.

### Missing Data

The absence of information was treated as a potential limitation rather than being silently filled with assumptions.

### Publication

Public-facing outputs remained subject to appropriate human approval before publication.

### Budget

Financial decisions and commitments remained under human authority.

### Human Intervention

The process allowed for escalation or intervention when outputs were unclear, contradictory, inappropriate, unsupported, or outside defined conditions.

---

## Stage 14 — Updated Learning Informing Future Decision-Making

The optimised process formally incorporated:

> **Stage 14 — Updated Learning Informing Future Decision-Making**

The purpose of this stage was to ensure that relevant observations from the process could inform future decisions and process improvements.

Learning could include:

* identified errors;
* unexpected outcomes;
* successful approaches;
* weak assumptions;
* evidence gaps;
* process inefficiencies;
* and opportunities for further testing.

The learning stage therefore transformed the process from a static workflow into a mechanism capable of informing subsequent iterations.

However, learning remained subject to human evaluation and governance.

The system was not assumed to automatically determine whether a learning was valid or whether it should be permanently incorporated.

---

## Process Time

The optimised process was modelled at approximately:

> **108 minutes**

Compared with the 464-minute manual baseline, this represents an estimated reduction of approximately:

> **76.7%**

Rounded to the nearest whole percentage, this is approximately **77%**.

The optimised process therefore retained almost all of the modelled capacity benefit demonstrated in Run B while introducing additional controls.

---

## Why Run C Took Slightly Longer Than Run B

Run B was modelled at approximately 107 minutes.

Run C was modelled at approximately 108 minutes.

The additional minute was not treated as a failure of optimisation.

Instead, it illustrated an important principle of the experiment:

> **Optimisation should not mean minimising time at all costs.**

Where additional process time is required to improve evidence checking, human review, governance, or risk control, that time may represent a worthwhile trade-off.

The goal is therefore not:

> **Minimum process time**

but:

> **Maximum useful capacity within acceptable quality and control boundaries.**

---

## Quality and Strategic Integrity

The optimised configuration maintained the requirement that strategic judgement remain human-led where appropriate.

Efficiency gains were therefore evaluated alongside:

* strategic quality;
* evidence quality;
* decision quality;
* brand integrity;
* human accountability;
* and risk management.

The optimisation process was considered successful only if capacity recovery could coexist with these requirements.

---

## Key Finding

Run C demonstrated that an AI-enabled process does not need to pursue maximum automation to achieve significant capacity benefits.

The experiment instead supports a more balanced model in which:

* AI handles suitable operational work;
* AI augments human analysis and execution;
* humans retain authority over consequential decisions;
* safeguards constrain inappropriate automation;
* and learning informs subsequent process decisions.

---

## Evidence Classification

Run C represents a **controlled/modelled optimised configuration**.

The approximately 108-minute process time demonstrates the modelled capacity effect of the refined process under the defined experimental conditions.

It does not establish production reliability, live system performance, or commercial impact.

---

## Final Finding From Run C

The most significant finding was not that AI could reduce the modelled process time by approximately 77%.

The more important finding was that **AI-enabled process optimisation should be treated as a systems-design problem rather than a simple automation exercise**.

The experiment therefore established a working principle for subsequent tests:

> **Efficiency is valuable only when it remains useful, controlled, explainable, and aligned with the objective of the work.**
