# Run B — AI-Engineered Process

## Experiment

**AI Automation Test #1 — Brand Awareness & Market Entry**

## Run Configuration

**Run:** B
**Configuration:** AI-Engineered Process
**Purpose:** Determine how the defined marketing process could be redesigned when AI was deliberately introduced as an operating capability.

---

## Process Objective

Run B used the same underlying marketing objective as Run A:

> **Building brand awareness through digital integration**

The purpose was to determine whether the process could be reorganised to reduce operational workload while preserving the strategic and decision-making responsibilities that should remain under human control.

The experiment therefore moved beyond asking:

> **"What tasks can AI perform?"**

and instead examined:

> **"How should the process change when AI becomes part of the operating environment?"**

---

## Starting Point

Run A established a manual baseline of approximately:

> **464 minutes**

The analysis of the baseline identified that human capacity was being consumed by a combination of:

* strategic decision-making;
* analysis;
* information processing;
* preparation;
* production;
* review;
* coordination;
* and administrative or repetitive activities.

The AI-engineering stage focused on distinguishing between these categories rather than treating the entire process as either human or automated.

---

## AI Allocation Model

Activities were evaluated according to three broad categories.

### 1. Automation

Activities that could potentially be delegated to AI under defined conditions and controls.

These included suitable repetitive, structured, information-processing, drafting, and preparation activities.

### 2. Augmentation

Activities where AI could accelerate or expand human capability while the human remained responsible for interpretation, judgement, or approval.

### 3. Human Authority

Activities requiring human ownership because they involved strategic judgement, accountability, ambiguity, risk, brand decisions, or consequential outcomes.

This allocation model became a core design principle of the experiment.

---

## Process Redesign

Rather than inserting AI into the existing manual workflow without modification, the process was deliberately restructured.

The redesigned process sought to:

* remove unnecessary manual repetition;
* shift suitable information-processing work to AI;
* use AI to accelerate preparation and analysis;
* preserve human review where judgement was required;
* maintain human authority over consequential decisions;
* and reduce the amount of human time spent on lower-value operational work.

The objective was therefore **process redesign**, not simply task automation.

---

## Human Role

Human involvement remained essential.

The human operator retained responsibility for:

* strategic interpretation;
* final decision-making;
* evaluation of ambiguous information;
* judgement where evidence was insufficient;
* brand considerations;
* risk assessment;
* approval;
* and accountability.

AI was therefore treated as an operating capability within the process rather than an autonomous replacement for the marketing function.

---

## AI Role

AI was used conceptually as a combination of:

* information-processing capability;
* analytical support;
* drafting and preparation capability;
* workflow acceleration;
* pattern identification;
* and decision-support.

The process design deliberately avoided granting AI unrestricted authority over strategic or consequential decisions.

---

## Process Time

The AI-engineered process was modelled at approximately:

> **107 minutes**

Compared with the Run A baseline of approximately 464 minutes, this represented an estimated:

> **77% reduction in process time**

The reduction was primarily attributable to restructuring the process so that suitable operational work could be handled or accelerated through AI rather than requiring equivalent manual effort.

---

## Capacity Effect

The modelled reduction indicated substantial potential recovery of human capacity.

The key implication was not simply that the process could be completed faster.

The more important finding was that skilled human capacity could potentially be redirected away from lower-value operational work toward activities such as:

* strategic judgement;
* interpretation;
* creative direction;
* risk assessment;
* stakeholder decisions;
* and higher-value problem solving.

This distinction became important when evaluating whether further optimisation should focus purely on reducing process time.

---

## Quality and Control

The AI-engineered configuration was evaluated against the same broader requirements as the manual baseline.

These included:

* strategic quality;
* decision quality;
* evidence quality;
* human oversight;
* brand integrity;
* and risk management.

The process therefore did not treat maximum automation as the desired outcome.

Where human judgement remained necessary, it was retained as part of the process design.

---

## Identified Risks

Introducing AI also created new potential risks.

These included:

* inaccurate outputs;
* insufficient evidence;
* overconfidence in AI-generated analysis;
* inappropriate automation;
* loss of contextual judgement;
* brand risk;
* incorrect interpretation;
* missing information;
* and excessive reliance on automated outputs.

These risks demonstrated that reducing process time alone could not determine whether the redesigned process was preferable.

---

## Need for Optimisation

Run B demonstrated the potential capacity benefit of deliberate AI integration.

However, it also highlighted an important limitation:

> **The fastest process is not necessarily the best process.**

Additional controls were required to determine where AI outputs should be checked, where human authority should remain explicit, and where safeguards should prevent efficiency from overriding quality or governance.

These findings informed the design of Run C.

---

## Evidence Classification

Run B represents a **controlled/modelled AI-engineered configuration**.

The approximately **107-minute** process time is therefore an experimental model rather than a validated production measurement.

The result demonstrates the potential capacity effect of process redesign under the defined experimental conditions.

It does not establish equivalent productivity in a live commercial environment.

---

## Key Finding From Run B

The principal finding from Run B was that meaningful AI value may emerge from **redesigning the operating process**, rather than simply automating individual marketing tasks.

The experiment therefore shifted the focus from:

> **AI as a task performer**

toward:

> **AI as a component of a human-AI operating system.**

This finding directly informed the optimisation performed in Run C.
